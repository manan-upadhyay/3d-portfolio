## 1. Where v1.0 landed

v1.0 shipped and got its first real audience: **~358 unique visitors in 3 days**,
**38% from Reddit**, **200+ qualitative comments**, and a full behavioral dataset.
That is a successful launch — enough signal to steer a real revamp.

**The one-sentence verdict from Beta 1:**

> The Chronicle is **memorable and distinctive** but **over-signals AI, overloads
> the reader, and converts poorly** — it wins attention and loses trust.

It **polarizes** rather than fails. The same site reads as "nice and functional,
skilled" to one segment and "AI slop" to another. v1.1's job is not to delete the
soul that earned the attention — it is to **raise the trust floor and the
conversion rate** so the skeptical segment also sees competence, and the
interested segment can actually act.

### The numbers that define the problem

| Metric | v1.0 baseline | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---|
| Avg max scroll depth | **41.8%** | Half the audience never sees projects or contact |
| Origin → About retention | **84%** (16% leave) | Biggest single funnel leak — the hero underperforms |
| Contact section reach | **36.6%** | Too few hiring-intent users reach the ask |
| Form abandonment | **78%** | The conversion funnel leaks at the final step |
| Overall lead conversion | **0.56%** (2 leads) | Below potential for this traffic quality |
| Windows form starts | **0** (from 21 reaching contact) | A platform bug is silently killing conversions |
| Device/browser null rate | **~90%** | Analytics instrumentation is partly blind |

## 2. The goal of v1.1

**Reposition from:**

> "A cinematic AI-looking portfolio with impressive details but confusing trust signals."

**To:**

> "A clear senior-developer portfolio with a memorable cinematic layer, real
> project proof, low-friction contact, and visible product thinking."

### Product north star

A visitor understands, within ~10 seconds and without hunting: **who** Manan is,
**what** role fits, **what stack**, **what he's actually shipped**, **why the site
itself is proof of craft**, and **how to reach him**. The Chronicle world remains —
as the *reward for exploring*, not the *toll gate before comprehension*.

### Design principle for the whole release

> **Restraint is the feature.** Senior taste shows through what you *don't* put on
> screen. v1.1 removes 30–50% of the default-path noise (copy, decorative motion,
> competing controls) and spends that budget on clarity and proof.

## 4. The plan of action (workstreams)

Each workstream lists its **purpose**, the **changes**, the **ROI thesis**, and
its **done bar**. Sequence top-to-bottom: A and B are release-blocking.

### Workstream A — Hero & trust repair *(P0, highest ROI)*

**Purpose.** The hero is the highest-leverage screen (biggest measured leak), and
the AI-perception + overload problems all concentrate there first. Fix the first
screen and the whole funnel lifts.

**Changes.**
- Rewrite `hero.*` in **all** voice bundles: value-first hook, action-descriptive
  CTAs ("See my work" / "Get in touch" / résumé), phrases that say what he builds.
- Add an above-the-fold **proof strip** (years · stack · shipped count · role).
- Point the primary CTA at **proof** (projects), not "about".
- Reduce astrolabe visual weight so it stops competing with the message.
- Remove the **AI visual tells**: redesign the uppercase `chapter-eyebrow`, break
  up pill/chip uniformity, and deliberately **vary section density** (uniform
  polish is itself an AI signal). Do a web search for "common signs of ai generated website", "AI website patterns", "why does my website look AI", "how to fix AI website look", and use that knowledge to fix the AI visual tells.
- Cut visible copy **50–70%** across every section; push depth behind disclosure.

**ROI thesis.** Improving Origin→About retention and scroll depth compounds
through every downstream section — this is the single change most likely to move
lead conversion. Removing the named AI tells directly attacks the #1 trust risk.

**Done bar.** A recruiter grasps role-fit from the hero alone; no tester calls the
first impression "AI slop"; avg scroll ≥ 50%; Origin→About retention ≥ 90%.

### Workstream B — Conversion recovery *(P0)*

**Purpose.** Warm traffic is arriving and leaking. 78% form abandonment and a
Windows funnel at zero are pure recoverable revenue.

**Changes.**
- Simplify the contact form (fewer fields; de-theme the copy: "Dispatch the
  Raven" → "Send message"); add a low-pressure "just say hi" path.
- Surface **quick-contact alternatives** (email copy, LinkedIn, GitHub, résumé)
  prominently — many warm leads never want a form.
- Add a **sticky/secondary CTA** after ~50–75% scroll / project view.
- Rework the footer into a real **final conversion scene**, not a dead end.
- **Debug the Windows Chrome/Edge contact bug** (z-index / pointer-events /
  Lenis focus-trap / custom-cursor overlay are the prime suspects).

**ROI thesis.** These are bottom-of-funnel fixes on already-interested users —
the highest conversion-per-effort in the release. The Windows bug alone is ~21
lost sessions per beta.

**Done bar.** Form start ≥ 5–8%; abandonment < 40%; Windows form starts > 0;
direct email/LinkedIn/GitHub clicks measurably up.

### Workstream C — Performance & instrumentation *(P0)*

**Purpose.** A portfolio that degrades over a session *disproves* the craft it
claims. And we cannot verify any v1.1 change worked while analytics are 90% blind.

**Changes.**
- Audit every `useEffect` / GSAP context / `requestAnimationFrame` / ScrollTrigger
  for cleanup (pinned horizontal Experience and the hero canvas are prime leak
  suspects); **cap canvas DPR at 2** (4K displays multiply cost).
- Profile a 3-minute session for growing heap / detached nodes / listener growth.
- Fix PostHog **super-property registration order** (register before first
  capture); add `beta_round` / `tracking_version`; add session **heartbeats**
  (15/30/60s) and full `/making-of` tracking; add the v1.1 event catalog
  (see action plan §14).

**ROI thesis.** Perf is a direct trust signal to the exact (developer) audience
that was most skeptical. Instrumentation is the prerequisite for judging Beta 2 —
without it the whole release is unmeasured.

**Done bar.** No measurable degradation after 3 min; DPR capped; zero leaked
contexts/listeners; device/browser null rate < 5%; session coverage ≥ 60%.

### Workstream D — Project proof *(P1)*

**Purpose.** "Zero images" is the sharpest, most repeated trust gap and the
biggest amplifier of the AI-slop perception. Proof serves *both* audiences — the
skeptics who want evidence and the impressed-but-uninformed who asked "which
skills did you use?"

**Changes.**
- Add real **screenshots / short silent loops** to every featured project.
- Reorder by demonstrated interest (**Gajaakriti Studio first** — 3× the clicks).
- Show 3 featured projects by default; the rest behind "Show more".
- Rewrite project copy to **proof bullets** (role, stack, decisions, outcome),
  not poetic description; make live-demo CTAs prominent.

**ROI thesis.** The single best anti-"AI-slop" move, and it lifts a section half
the audience currently never reaches.

**Done bar.** Every featured project has a real screenshot; project section reach
≥ 60%; project clicks ≥ 25 per ~350 visitors; "zero images" comments gone.

### Workstream E — Clarity & polish *(P1 → P2)*

**Purpose.** Clean up the friction that makes the experience feel confusing or
heavy without touching the soul.

**Changes.**
- **Label every persistent control** (Map/Theme/Sound/Voice); collapse mobile's
  floating cluster into one menu; separate brand mark from the map/nav icon.
- **Reduce Lenis smoothing**; use native scroll on mobile / reduced-motion; make
  the horizontal Experience timeline accept horizontal + keyboard input.
- **Simplify or default-off the custom cursor**; disable on touch/reduced-motion.
- Give the **astrolabe** a real job or make it clearly decorative and smaller.
- Rebuild **`/making-of`** from cinematic self-narration into a real engineering
  case study (architecture, decisions, "what beta users said / what I changed",
  perf + a11y notes) with a closing CTA.
- Add **security headers** to `vercel.json` (an easy credibility win).
- Larger mobile tap targets (≥44px), better decorative-heading letter-spacing.

**ROI thesis.** Removes the "confusing / scary to click / buggy" friction that
costs mid-funnel retention, and turns `/making-of` from a liability into proof.

**Done bar.** New users identify every control without guessing; no "buggy scroll"
comments; mobile user finds projects/résumé/contact in <10s; `/making-of` reads
as a case study, not a victory lap.

## 6. Expected output of v1.1

When v1.1 ships, the site should demonstrably:

1. **Communicate value in one screen** — role, stack, proof, and a clear next
   action above the fold, in every voice.
2. **Read as human-authored** — no uppercase-eyebrow / pill-uniform AI tells;
   varied density; copy cut by half; Chronicle flavor as seasoning.
3. **Prove the work** — real screenshots and proof bullets on every featured
   project, ordered by interest.
4. **Convert without friction** — a simple form, prominent direct-contact
   options, a sticky CTA, a footer that closes, and a **working Windows funnel**.
5. **Perform** — stable over a long session, DPR-capped, leak-free.
6. **Measure itself** — reliable device data, full event catalog, `/making-of`
   instrumented, so Beta 2 is judged on data, not vibes.
7. **Keep its soul** — the Chronicle world, themes, sound, map, and easter eggs
   survive as the optional reward layer.

**Definition of done for the release:** every workstream's done bar is met; the
per-section DoD in `CLAUDE.md §4` holds (responsive 360/768/1280/1920, dark+light,
reduced-motion + touch, no console errors, clean `npm run build`); and the three
Beta-1 reports' P0 items are all closed.

## 7. What v1.1 must NOT do

- **Don't delete the personality.** The Chronicle concept, atmosphere, themes,
  sound, and map are the differentiators users *praised* — and the reason the
  site got 200+ comments instead of being ignored. Restraint, not removal.
- **Don't get defensive about AI.** The fix is specificity and proof, not a
  disclaimer.
- **Don't solve confusion with more words.** Solve it with labels, hierarchy, and
  affordances.
- **Don't chase any single loud opinion.** Ship what the clusters *and* the
  analytics agree on.

## 11. Interaction & mobile standards (v1.1 addendum)

> Three standing rules, each with a **site-wide audit tracker** (§11.4). These
> join `CLAUDE.md §4` and the [Anti-Slop Codex](common-ai-signs.md) as
> definition-of-done gates for every page — run a section against them before
> calling it done, and update the tracker in the same change.

### 11.1 Rule — Affordance clarity ("it looks like what it does")

- **Actions look like controls; text never looks like an action.** Anything
  clickable must read as a control (button/link chrome + cursor + hover/press/
  focus feedback). Plain text must never masquerade as a control — no fake
  buttons, no arrow-on-text that isn't a link, no `data-cursor="hover"` on
  non-interactive elements. Use real `<button>` / `<a>`.
- **Inform subtly, without prose.** Communicate an interaction or state with the
  *lightest* sufficient cue — an icon, a micro-animation, a peek, a progress bar,
  a counter, or an **industry-proven pattern users are already trained on**
  (carousel arrows, a page dot, a disclosure caret). Reach for text only when
  nothing else will do, and then keep it to a few words.
- **The test:** a first-time visitor should know what every element does at a
  glance, and never tap something "to see what happens."

### 11.2 Rule — Mobile popover / hover parity

- **Every hover-driven UI works on touch, in a single tap.** Tooltips, info
  popovers (`Hovercard` / `Marginalia`), NDA notes, menus, cards: one tap opens
  (never a double-tap; don't also open on `focus`, which fights the tap). Mouse
  keeps hover; keyboard opens with Enter/Space; outside-tap + Escape close.
- **Popovers are clamped to the viewport** — never off-screen (compute against
  `innerWidth`, flip above/below near edges).
- Every desktop interaction has a defined coarse-pointer/touch equivalent;
  `prefers-reduced-motion` + `(hover:none)` honored (`CLAUDE.md §4.5`).

### 11.3 Rule — A dedicated mobile experience (not a responsive shrink)

- Mobile is **its own designed experience** — clean, human, cinematic, with the
  sound design and a user-first clarity — **not** a squeezed desktop. Layouts,
  controls, and interactions may **diverge** from desktop where the phone
  deserves a better answer (e.g. the single `MobileMenu`, the Experience floating
  carousel arrows, native scroll, the decluttered mobile map, mobile-silent
  arsenal hum). Ask per section: "what's the *right* phone experience?", not
  "how does the desktop reflow?".
